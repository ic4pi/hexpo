01 — Foundation

What Is
Novelty Theory?

Terence McKenna proposed that the universe has a fundamental drive toward novelty — the emergence of new patterns, forms, and complexity over time. This is not a metaphor. It is, he argued, a measurable, mathematical property of reality itself.

Novelty Theory (also called Timewave Zero or the Eschaton hypothesis) proposes that time is not a neutral medium through which events flow — it has a texture, oscillating between periods of ingression of novelty (maximum complexity and connection) and habit (repetition and stasis).

McKenna derived this from the structure of the ancient I Ching — the 4,000-year-old Chinese oracle — claiming its 64 hexagrams encode a fractal map of time's unfolding.

🌀

Novelty

The degree of connectedness and complexity in a moment. High novelty = maximum possibility; the universe is most "itself." Low novelty = repetition; habit dominates over change.

♾️

Fractal Time

The pattern of novelty repeats at every scale — days mirror years, years mirror centuries, centuries mirror ages — like a fractal coastline repeating its shape at every zoom level.

🎯

The Eschaton

The wave asymptotically approaches infinite novelty at a specific point in time — a singularity McKenna originally placed at December 21, 2012. Reality itself "ends" by becoming wholly novel.

🔮

I Ching Origin

McKenna studied the 64 hexagrams and their 384 lines. He found a mathematical relationship between them that, when graphed, produced a wave mirroring historical novelty peaks and troughs.

🌊

Historical Resonance

McKenna overlaid the wave onto history and claimed peaks of novelty matched the Renaissance, WWII, 9/11, and technological accelerations — while troughs matched Dark Ages and stagnation.

⚗️

The Role of Mind

Consciousness itself is novelty incarnate. As the universe becomes more novel, it necessarily produces more complex forms of mind — culminating in a kind of universal awakening.

02 — The Mathematics

The Math
Behind the Wave

McKenna, with his brother Dennis, developed a mathematical framework from the King Wen sequence of the I Ching. The resulting function generates what he called the Timewave — a fractal, self-similar wave of novelty through time.

Step 1 — The I Ching Difference Array

Start with the 64 hexagrams of the King Wen sequence. For each adjacent pair of hexagrams, count the number of lines that differ (0–6). This produces a sequence of 64 difference values, called W(n).

W(n) = |lines_changed(hexagram_n → hexagram_{n+1})|
n ranges from 1 to 64 (with wraparound); each value ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

McKenna claimed this sequence, when plotted and analyzed, reveals a hidden mathematical structure that reflects the flow of time's novelty. The 384 total lines in the I Ching (64 hexagrams × 6 lines) became foundational — 384 also appears in lunar calendrics.

Step 2 — Generating the Wave Values

McKenna derived a novelty value N(t) for any moment t in time. Lower N(t) means MORE novelty; higher N(t) means MORE habit. (Counter-intuitive: novelty is measured by the inverse of the wave height.)

N(t) = Σ W(n) · f(t, n, scale)
Where scale determines which temporal period is mapped

The Three-Level Construction

The Timewave is not a single wave but a hierarchical composite of three mathematical transformations of the difference array:

LEVEL 1 — TRIGRAMMATIC
T(n) = W(n) + W(n + 32) [mod 64]

The first 32 values are added to the last 32 values, creating a 32-element array. This models the interaction of the first and second half of the hexagram sequence.

LEVEL 2 — HEXAGRAMMATIC
H(n) = T(n) + T(n + 16) [mod 32]

The process continues, folding the 32-element array into 16 elements. This captures the hexagram-trigram relationship in the I Ching structure.

LEVEL 3 — COMPOSITE WAVE
TWZ(t) = ∫₀ᵗ [T(x) + H(x) + W(x)] dx · e^(-λt)

All three levels are summed and integrated to produce the final Timewave function. The exponential term causes the wave to amplify as it approaches the zero point.

The Fractal Self-Similarity Principle

The Timewave is fractal: the same pattern repeats at different temporal scales, each 64× smaller than the last. McKenna identified six levels of scale:

Scale Level Period Duration Historical Analog Novelty Character
Cosmic 72 billion years Age of the universe × ~5 Emergence of matter, stars, galaxies
Biological 1.3 billion years Rise of complex life Cambrian explosion, evolution of nervous systems
Civilizational ~67,000 years Modern human emergence Language, art, ritual, agricultural revolution
Historical ~4,300 years Bronze Age to present Empires, religions, philosophy, science
Cultural ~67 years Within a human lifetime WWII, Cold War, Digital Age
Personal ~384 days Just over a year Seasonal and personal cycles of change

The Scaling Ratio

Each level is exactly 64× larger than the one below it — the same number as the I Ching hexagrams. This ratio is the core fractal resonance:

Scale_{n+1} = Scale_n × 64

This means a single day within the personal scale mirrors an entire civilization-scale epoch. The same events — metaphorically — happen at every level, with the same proportional timing of novelty peaks and troughs.

Timewave Zero — The Singularity Condition

As t approaches the terminal date, the Timewave function approaches zero asymptotically. This represents a state of infinite novelty — a phase transition in which the universe transcends its own structure.

lim(t → t₀) TWZ(t) = 0
Where t₀ is the terminal date (McKenna's original: Dec 21, 2012)

The wave never actually reaches zero — it's asymptotic. McKenna interpreted this mathematically as the collapse of the distinction between possibility and actuality. Every potential pattern becomes simultaneously real.

Why December 21, 2012?

McKenna anchored the wave by aligning its major novelty peaks with historical events he could verify — primarily the dropping of the atomic bomb (August 6, 1945), which he identified as the single most novel event of the 20th century.

Working backward from this anchor using the fractal ratios, the zero point fell on December 21, 2012 — which coincidentally aligned with the end of the Mayan Long Count calendar. McKenna saw this as confirmation.

t₀ = Aug 6, 1945 + (scale × resonance factor) = Dec 21, 2012

Note: 2012 has passed without the predicted singularity. McKenna himself acknowledged the theory was speculative and that the zero date could be recalibrated. The theory's value lies more in its conceptual framework than predictive accuracy.

04 — Study Guide

Master the Theory

Core Concepts
Glossary
Study Roadmap

🌀 The Core Claim

Time is not neutral. It has a measurable "texture" that alternates between novelty (complexity, connection, newness) and habit (repetition, stasis, predictability). This texture follows a mathematical pattern derivable from the I Ching.

📐 The Mathematical Backbone

The Timewave is computed from the King Wen sequence of I Ching hexagrams. Their line-change differences produce a numerical array. This array is transformed at three levels and combined to produce a fractal wave function. Lower wave values = more novelty.

🔄 The Fractal Structure

The same pattern repeats at 6 scales, each 64× larger than the last (cosmic → biological → civilizational → historical → cultural → personal). Events at one scale are metaphorical echoes of events at every other scale.

🎯 The Eschaton

All lines of novelty converge on a terminal point where the wave approaches zero — infinite novelty. McKenna called this the Eschaton or Omega Point. He originally dated it to Dec 21, 2012, though he acknowledged the date was speculative and the theory's real value was philosophical-mathematical.

🧠 What McKenna Meant by "Novelty"

Novelty ≠ merely "new things." It means an increase in the density of connection — more relationships, more complexity, more integration across a system. The internet, for example, is not novel because it's new, but because it dramatically increases the number of connections between minds, ideas, and information.

Novelty ▾
The degree of connectedness, complexity, and newness in any given moment. McKenna used it technically to mean the density of causal connections and the emergence of genuinely new patterns that have not existed before.
Habit ▾
The opposite of novelty — repetition, stasis, and the dominance of established patterns over new ones. Periods of high habit are characterized by conservatism, slow change, and rote behavior at civilizational scales.
Timewave Zero ▾
The mathematical function McKenna developed, also the name of the software he used to display it. "Zero" refers to the terminal point where the wave asymptotically approaches zero — infinite novelty — at the end of time.
King Wen Sequence ▾
The traditional ordering of the 64 I Ching hexagrams, attributed to King Wen of Zhou (c. 1100 BCE). McKenna used the mathematical relationships between adjacent hexagrams in this sequence as the raw data for his wave function.
Ingression of Novelty ▾
McKenna's phrase for a moment when novelty "enters" the world — a peak of connectivity, creativity, or transformation. These moments are mapped to the valleys (low points) of the Timewave graph.
Eschaton ▾
From Greek "éskhatos" (last). The terminal point of history — the attractor toward which all time flows. McKenna saw it as a kind of universal phase transition, not "the end of the world" but the end of time as a linear medium.
Fractal Resonance ▾
The property by which the same Timewave pattern appears at every scale of time. A "novelty peak" that occurs over the course of a year will have a mirroring structure within a single day within that year, scaled by the ratio of 64.
Omega Point ▾
A term McKenna borrowed from Jesuit philosopher Teilhard de Chardin — the convergence point of universal evolution toward maximum complexity and consciousness. McKenna equated it with his Eschaton and Timewave Zero.
Difference Array W(n) ▾
The foundational numerical sequence in McKenna's math. For each pair of adjacent I Ching hexagrams, it records how many of the 6 lines differ. This 64-element array is the raw data from which the entire Timewave is constructed.
Temporal Texture ▾
McKenna's metaphor for the quality of time at any given moment — some moments feel "thick" with possibility and change; others feel "thin" and static. The Timewave attempts to quantify this subjective sense into an objective mathematical function.

  • Understand the Core Claim

    Novelty Theory asserts that time is not neutral — it has a measurable quality (novelty vs. habit) that follows a mathematical pattern. This is the philosophical foundation. Grasp this before anything else.

  • Learn the I Ching Basics

    You don't need to master the I Ching, but understand that it consists of 64 hexagrams (6-line symbols), each made of two trigrams (3-line symbols). The King Wen sequence orders these 64 hexagrams in a specific way.

  • Grasp the Difference Array

    For each adjacent pair of hexagrams in the King Wen sequence, count how many of the 6 lines change. This 64-number sequence (W(n)) is the mathematical raw material. Values range from 0 to 6.

  • Understand the Three-Level Transformation

    The difference array is transformed at three levels (trigrammatic, hexagrammatic, composite) by folding it on itself and summing. This produces a 384-point wave function — one point for each line in the I Ching.

  • Master the Fractal Scaling

    The 384-point wave is mapped to time at six nested scales, each 64× larger. Learn the six scales: personal (384 days), cultural (67 years), historical (4,300 years), civilizational (67,000 years), biological (1.3 billion years), cosmic (72 billion years).

  • Understand the Zero Condition

    As time approaches the terminal date, all scales of the wave simultaneously approach zero (their minimum, maximum novelty). This is the Eschaton — a mathematical singularity in which the wave can no longer progress forward in time.

  • Evaluate the Theory Critically

    Consider both McKenna's arguments and his critics. The wave's mapping to history is not reproducible in a scientific sense. The 2012 prediction did not come true. Yet the conceptual framework — that novelty is increasing and accelerating — remains compelling and debated.
